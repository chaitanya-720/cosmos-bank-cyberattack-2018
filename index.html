02OVERVIEW

Executive Summary

In August 2018, Cosmos Cooperative Bank reported a major cyber incident involving fraudulent ATM-related transactions and unauthorized transfer activity linked to SWIFT-related operations. Public reporting indicates that attackers abused the bank's transaction processing environment to move funds rapidly across multiple channels. The incident highlighted how cyber intrusion and financial fraud can converge in modern banking systems. Reported impact values vary by source, so this presentation uses clearly attributed references for factual claims.

03TARGET

About the Target

Cosmos Cooperative Bank was a long-running Indian cooperative bank serving retail and commercial banking needs. As with many financial institutions, its operations depended on interconnected transaction systems, card channels, and international payment interfaces.

Banking infrastructure is a high-value cybersecurity target because disruption or manipulation can lead directly to financial loss, customer trust impact, and systemic risk.

07ATM FRAUD

ATM / Card Transaction Attack

  • Public reporting describes coordinated fraudulent ATM withdrawals executed in a short time window.
  • Simultaneous activity suggests orchestration rather than isolated card misuse.
  • Abuse of transaction infrastructure can bypass assumptions in normal daily fraud baselines.
  • Behavior-based monitoring is critical for detecting high-rate and geographically distributed anomalies.
08INTL TRANSFERS

International Transfer / SWIFT-related Phase

SWIFT is a financial messaging network. In this case study, phrasing is deliberately precise: fraudulent transactions involving the bank's SWIFT-related operations, rather than claiming SWIFT itself was hacked.
